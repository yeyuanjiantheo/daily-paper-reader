# Short-term barrier option price expansion 

Masaaki Fukasawa *

Abstract 

We derive a short-maturity expansion for up-and-out put barrier option prices under continuous stochastic volatility when the strike and the barrier approach the spot at the di ff usive scale. Assuming joint weak convergence of the normalized terminal return, the relative volatility fluctuation, and the running maximum, together with uniform integrability, we show that the leading term is the time-inhomogeneous Black–Scholes barrier price fitted to the forward variance curve. The first model-dependent correction is of order θH+1/2, where θH , H ∈ (0 , 1/2], is the order of the relative volatility fluctuation, and is represented explicitly through killed Brownian transition densities. For regular volatility models, where H = 1/2, the coe ffi cient is determined by the short-maturity at-the-money implied-volatility skew. For rough volatility models with H < 1/2, the coe ffi cient reduces to a one-dimensional integral. Numerical experiments show that the correction materially improves the Black–Scholes approximation. 

Keywords: barrier option; short-term asymptotics; rough volatility. 

# 1 Introduction 

Barrier options are arguably the most basic class of exotic derivatives. They are among the simplest path-dependent claims, but already exhibit the main di ffi culty that distinguishes exotic from European option pricing: their value depends not only on the terminal distribution of the underlying asset but also on the behavior of the price path before maturity. Understanding barrier options is therefore a natural first step toward a short-maturity asymptotic theory for more general path-dependent derivatives. Despite their basic role, rigorous asymptotic results for barrier option prices appear to be relatively scarce compared with the extensive literature on Euro-pean options. One important line of research uses singu-lar perturbation methods. Ilhan et al. [10] extended the fast mean-reverting stochastic-volatility approach of Fouque et al. [4] to barrier and other exotic op-tions. In the presence of an absorbing boundary, the usual Black–Scholes Greek correction acquires an additional boundary contribution. Fouque et al. [5] sub-sequently developed a spectral perturbation approach to fast mean-reverting stochastic volatility, obtaining approximations for up-and-out, double-barrier knock-out, and rebate options. These works demonstrate in particular that the barrier creates a genuine singular-perturbation issue that is absent for European claims. Other asymptotic approaches are also available. Hu and Knessl [9] obtained a singular-perturbation expansion for barrier prices under the CEV model. Kato et al. [11] developed a semigroup expansion for the Cauchy–Dirichlet problem associated with continuously monitored barrier options and applied it to regular di ff usion-type stochastic volatility models, including SABR. Their semigroup approach provides a mathematically rigorous justification of the perturbation expansion under suitable regularity conditions. These works expand with respect to a model parameter while maturity is fixed. Our concern is di ff erent. We derive a short-maturity expansion without introducing a perturbation parameter into the model. The barrier itself must then be scaled with maturity. Indeed, if the barrier is fixed away from the spot, hitting it becomes a rare event as maturity tends to zero. We instead consider the near-barrier regime in which the distance between the barrier and the spot is of the same order as the typical short-time price fluctuation. More precisely, for maturity θ ↓ 0, initial variance v0, and fixed normalized levels b > max {k, 0},we set 

B = S0(1 + pv0θ, b), K = S0(1 + pv0θ, k).

This joint scaling keeps both the strike and the barrier visible in the short-time limit and leads to a Bachelier barrier problem at leading order. Recent work on the short-time behavior of up-and-in options with a barrier fixed away from the spot [3] concerns the complementary rare-event regime. The main contribution of this paper is to provide a rigorous short-maturity expansion in this non-degenerate near-barrier regime under minimal assump-tions. In particular, we obtain an explicit remainder o(θH+1/2) after the first stochastic-volatility correction. Thus, in contrast with much of the existing bar-rier asymptotics, where expansions are derived through model-specific pertur-bation arguments, our result gives a rigorous error estimate under an abstract probabilistic limit assumption and applies both to regular and rough volatility models.
