# On the Pricing of American Options under Stochastic Local Volatility and Stochastic Correlation via the RBSDE Framework 

L. Teng ∗

Applied and Computational Mathematics University of Wuppertal Wuppertal, Germany October 2, 2026 

Abstract 

In this work, we study the pricing of American options under stochastic local volatility (SLV) models extended by including stochastic correlation driven by an additional stochastic process. We generalize the class of SLV models by incorporating a flexible stochastic correlation structure. To price options within these extended models, we derive the correspond-ing reflected forward-backward stochastic differential equations (RBSDEs) and employ data-driven numerical methods to solve them for both pricing and hedging purposes. The RBSDE framework enables the modelling of the future evolution of the option price. Furthermore, we conduct a con-vergence analysis of the proposed numerical method and present numer-ical experiments that illustrate the performance of the extended models, as well as the accuracy and efficiency of the RBSDE-based approach. 

Keywords: Stochastic local volatility, Stochastic correlation, American op-tions, Reflected forward-backward stochastic differential equations (RBSDEs) 

## 1 Introduction 

Local volatility (LV) models were proposed in Dupire (1994); Derman and Kani (1994) for an exact and fast fit to the implied volatility surface of European option prices with the Dupire’s formula. However, it has been pointed out in Hagen et al. (2002) that these self-consistent and arbitrage-free models predict the wrong dynamics of the implied volatility curve, leading to unstable hedges of exotic options. An alternative class of parametric models are stochastic volatil-ity (SV) models, e.g. Heston (1993); Hull and White (1987), which can provide 

> ∗

Email: teng@math.uni-wuppertal.de 

1arXiv:2610.01187v1 [q-fin.CP] 1 Oct 2026 a more realistic term structure for implied volatility surface, in particular future smiles. Stochastic local volatility (SLV) models can be constructed by combing desirable features from both LV models and SV models, the well-known example is the stochastic Alpha Beta Rho (SABR) model Hagen et al. (2002). Despite those features of the SLV models, it is quite challenging to compute option prices and hedging quantities due to the correlated Brownian motions and the nonlinear local volatility structure in the price process. In the literature so far, several Monte Carlo simulation methods have been proposed; see, e.g., Cai et al. (2017); Chen et al. (2012); Leitao et al. (2017) and references therein. Another class of methods is to use asymptotic expansion techniques Antonov et al. (2013); Armstrong et al. (2018); Hagen et al. (2002) and many others. The performance of the pricing using these methods is not consistent while varying all parameters, e.g., it is well known that the expansion of implied volatility in Hagen et al. (2002) is less accurate for a large maturity, or when the options are deep in or out of the money. This is one of the reasons why a double layer continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) approximation method is proposed in Cui et al. (2018), in which the pricing of American options is also considered. This CTMC approximation method is more general and applicable for many SLV models with an arbitrary correlation structure. In Cui et al. (2017), a transform-based method is proposed to price swaps and options related to dis-cretely sampled realized variance under a general class of stochastic volatility models with jumps, where a CTMC weak approximation scheme is utilized for the variance process. To the best of our knowledge, SLV models in the existing literature typically assume constant correlations. However, there is extensive empirical evidence Driessen et al. (2009); Teng et al. (2016a,b,c, 2018), and references therein, including that correlations between financial variables - such as between asset returns or between asset prices and their volatilities - are inherently stochastic. Clearly, assuming constant correlations may lead to misestimation and introduce correlation risk. In this paper, we propose an extension of standard SLV models extended by incorporating a flexible stochastic correlation structure, resulting in what we refer to as stochastic correlation local volatility (SCLV) models. For pricing options European-style options under the SCLV framework - which leads to a three-dimensional problem even for a single underlying asset - methods such as Monte Carlo simulation (in combination with backward dynamic programming) or Fourier transform techniques may be applicable. However, these methods are not well-suited for pricing American-style options, due to the early exercise feature and the increased model complexity. For instance, the commonly used double-layer CTMC approximation would need to be extended to a triple-layer structure to accommodate the stochastic correlation, which poses significant computational challenges. Moreover, deriving hedge parameters with respect to the local volatility, stochastic correlation, and asset price is generally non-trivial within these frameworks. Therefore, to address these challenges, we employ an RBSDE-based ap-proach. This framework provides a natural and flexible methodology for han-2dling early-exercise features and the multidimensional structure of the SCLV model. Importantly, the RBSDE formulation in our approach not only facili-tates the computation of option prices and their sensitivities, but also enables modelling of the future evolution of option prices in a dynamic and consistent manner. Research on the pricing of high-dimensional American options remains an active area in the literature; see, for example, Lelong (2018); Redmann (2023); Yang and Li (2024b). In particular, several state-of-the-art deep learning-based approaches have been proposed. A deep learning method for optimal stop-ping problems was first introduced in the pioneering work Becker et al. (2019), while approaches based on deep neural networks (DNNs) for computing stop-ping boundaries were first studied in Reppen et al. (2025), see also Soner and Tissot-Daguette (2025). Many other related contributions, such as Bayraktar et al. (2024); Becker et al. (2021); Dong (2024); Hur´ e et al. (2020); Reppen et al. (2023); Yang and Li (2024a), can be employed for pricing American op-tions under the SCLV models. The deep primal-dual algorithm introduced in the recent work Yang and Li (2024a) provides both lower and upper bounds for American option prices and is therefore naturally chosen as the benchmark for our approach. We summarize the main contributions of this work as follows: 1. We extend classical SLV models by introducing a flexible stochastic correla-tion structure, governed by a suitable stochastic process, resulting in the SCLV model. This generalization enables effective hedging of correlation risk. Within this framework, we derive the RBSDE pricing and hedging formulas (with re-spect to the asset price, local volatility, and correlation) for American options. 2. The proposed RBSDE framework serves as a general and flexible valuation methodology for SCLV models. The proposed RBSDE algorithm achieves high numerical accuracy, as demonstrated through comparisons with the state-of-the-art methods. In addition, we provide a rigorous convergence analysis to support its reliability. Beyond valuation, the RBSDE framework also enables the modelling of the future evolution of the option prices and hedging strategies, making it a powerful tool for both pricing and risk management. 3. To the best of our knowledge, pricing and delta hedging of American options under combined stochastic local volatility and stochastic correlation have not been previously studied. Our results therefore establish important benchmarks for future research in this area. In the next section, we start with extension of the SLV models by including a stochastic correlation structure, aiming to develop the SCLV models. In Sec-tion 3 we introduce the RBSDEs for pricing American-type options in the SCLV models. Section 4 is devoted to introduction to the XGBoost regression-based scheme including error analysis. The applications of the developed RBSDE pricing framework to the classical SABR and Heston stochastic local volatility (HSLV) models extended with stochastic correlation structure are addressed in Section 5. In Section 6, we provide a set of numerical experiments for Amer-ican options to illustrate the accuracy and efficiency of the proposed RBSDE approach. Finally, Section 7 concludes this work. 32 Extension of general stochastic local volatility model with stochastic correlation

## 4 Regression-based numerical schemes 

For the previously developed RBSDE pricing framework, we construct a gradient boosting-based backward approximation algorithm. The main idea is to first dis-cretize the time integrands using, for example, the general theta-discretization method Zhao et al. (2009, 2012), and then approximate the resulting condi-tional expectations via regression-based techniques. While related regression-based algorithms for standard BSDEs have been proposed in Teng (2021, 2022), the numerical treatment of RBSDEs considered here is fundamentally different due to the presence of the reflection mechanism and the associated obstacle constraint. Consequently, both the semi-discrete and fully discrete schemes de-veloped in the following section require a different construction from those for standard BSDEs. Moreover, the convergence analysis for the RBSDE schemes is new and substantially more challenging, as it must explicitly account for the additional errors induced by the reflection and obstacle approximation. We therefore provide a dedicated rigorous convergence analysis for the proposed RBSDE discretizations.

## 6 Numerical experiments 

In this section, we present numerical results pertaining to the pricing of Amer-ican options contracts within the SCLV models. The intention is to illustrate the applicability of the proposed RBSDE approach and its efficiency, and to see the role of stochastic correlation on pricing and hedging. In all numerical exper-iments, we employ the following XGBoost parameter settings unless otherwise specified: the maximum tree depth is set to ed = 2; the learning rate to η = 0 .9(with a low tree depth and a large learning rate chosen to expedite computation when possible), datasets of size M are split into training and test sets with a ration of 3 : 1 , the number of trees is set to be 150 , and the “Early Stopping” technique is applied with early stopping rounds set to 1 . For all remaining parameters, default values are employed. We perform 10 Picard iterations to solve the implicit equation for Y -component. As indicated in Teng (2022), comparable accuracy levels can be attained across a range of standard learning rates (i.e., between 0 and 1) by adjusting the number of trees for general problems. Nevertheless, challenging problems 22 necessitate a smaller learning rate. In Teng (2022), the number of trees is deter-mined through manual comparison of training and test curves, and this value is applied consistently across all time steps. In contrast to this methodology, we employ “Early Stopping” to automatically determine the specific number of trees at each time step. In the case of a standard BSDE (i.e., dX t = dW t), there is an absence of forward discretization error. Moreover, our SCLV models are formulated using decoupled forward BSDEs, where the underlying processes Xt are forward SDEs. These forward processes need to be numerically simulated, e.g., with the Euler-Maruyama scheme, when their analytical solutions are unavailable. The algorithms are executed independently ten times, and the average value is computed for approximation. Different seeds are used for each simulation. ## 7 Conclusion 

One method to hedge correlation risk is to incorporate stochastic correlation into pricing and hedging strategies. This work studies the pricing of American options under SLV models, extended by introducing stochastic correlation driven by a stochastic process, referred to as the SCLV models. The SLV models already pose significant challenges for standard pricing techniques, and these challenges are further compounded in the SCLV framework. To address these challenges, we propose an RBSDE–based approach for pric-ing and hedging American options under stochastic correlation, volatility, and spot price dynamics. A key advantage of the RBSDE methodology is that it also enables computation of the future evolution of the option price. Comparisons with state-of-the-art deep learning methods demonstrate the effectiveness of the RBSDE approach, the high accuracy of the regression-based algorithm, and the significant impact of stochastic correlation on both pricing and delta-hedging. Future research directions include addressing the challenges posed by ill-conditioned systems of equations, with the goal of obtaining robust numerical approximations for hedging under stochastic volatility and correlation. More-over, there is a growing need for faster numerical methods for solving RBSDEs, particularly to enable efficient calibration of SCLV models.
